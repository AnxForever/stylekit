# StyleKit product context

## Register

product

## Users

- Developers using AI coding tools who need concrete, reusable UI constraints.
- Designers and frontend developers comparing visual directions and implementation examples.
- Independent contributors sharing original styles and discussing their implementation.
- English- and Chinese-speaking readers; some editorial resources remain English-only.

## Product Purpose

Help people choose a visual direction, inspect its examples, and carry its tokens,
recipes, and prompts into their own project. Community participation should connect
real people and their work, not manufacture activity or imply that a prompt alone
guarantees a production-ready interface.

## Brand Personality

Practical, precise, restrained. Preserve the existing public site's typography,
neutral surfaces, and selective accent color. Individual style showcases, not the
product controls, are where distinct visual identities belong.

## Anti-references

- Invented engagement, testimonials, rankings, or promises of guaranteed results.
- Decorative dashboards that conceal empty, failed, or unavailable data.
- Generic promotional card grids in place of useful browsing and participation.
- An unrelated visual redesign of established product chrome.

## Design Principles

1. Show usable examples and implementation constraints before marketing claims.
2. Make every empty state explain a useful next action.
3. Distinguish missing data from zero activity; make recovery visible.
4. Keep discovery, attribution, discussion, and contribution connected.
5. Keep public content, structured data, and machine-readable references consistent.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use semantic headings, labeled form controls, keyboard-operable interactions,
visible focus, accessible status messages, and reduced-motion alternatives.
Preserve locale and destination during authentication. Reuse the existing Radix
primitives; do not introduce native selects into product chrome.
